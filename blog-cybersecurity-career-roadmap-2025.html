Cybersecurity Career Roadmap for 2025

Cybersecurity ROSE Tech Academy · 2025-02-01 · 8 min read

Cybersecurity is one of the fastest-growing fields in technology. With millions of unfilled positions worldwide and salaries that consistently outpace other IT disciplines, the profession offers exceptional opportunity — but the path in can feel confusing. This roadmap breaks the journey into three clear stages: foundational, intermediate, and advanced.

Stage 1: Build the Foundation (0–12 months)

Cybersecurity is not truly an entry-level field — most practitioners come from IT support, networking, or software development. If you are starting from scratch, spend your first six to twelve months building foundational IT knowledge.

  • CompTIA A+ — Covers hardware, operating systems, networking, and troubleshooting. The gold standard for IT fundamentals.
  • CompTIA Network+ — Deep dive into networking concepts: TCP/IP, routing, switching, VPNs, and wireless. Essential for understanding how attackers and defenders think.
  • Microsoft IT Support Fundamentals — Free and beginner-friendly via Microsoft Learn.

Alongside certifications, set up a home lab. A basic lab with an old PC running VirtualBox, a Kali Linux VM, and a Windows Server VM gives you a safe environment to experiment with real tools.

Stage 2: Enter Security (Year 1–2)

Once you have IT fundamentals in place, CompTIA Security+ is the natural next step. It is the most widely recognised entry-level security certification, required by the U.S. Department of Defense and sought by employers across industries.

🎯 Key Certifications at This Stage

CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701), CompTIA CySA+ (cybersecurity analyst), and EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) are the most valuable credentials for landing your first security role.

At this stage, also develop skills in:

  • SIEM tools (Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel)
  • Vulnerability scanning (Nessus, OpenVAS)
  • Incident response procedures
  • Security frameworks (NIST, ISO 27001, CIS Controls)
  • Python scripting for automation

Entry-Level Role Targets

With Security+ in hand and some project experience, target these roles:

  • Security Operations Center (SOC) Analyst Tier 1
  • Information Security Analyst
  • Junior Penetration Tester
  • IT Security Specialist

SOC Analyst is the most common entry path. Many teams hire with Security+ alone and expect you to learn on the job. Tier 1 SOC roles involve monitoring alerts, triaging incidents, and escalating to senior analysts.

Stage 3: Specialise and Advance (Year 2–5+)

After two to three years in a security role, you are ready to specialise. The main specialisations in cybersecurity are:

  • Penetration Testing / Red Team — Offensive security. Certifications: OSCP, CEH, GPEN.
  • Security Operations / Blue Team — Defensive security and incident response. Certifications: CySA+, GCIH, GCIA.
  • Cloud Security — Securing cloud environments. Certifications: CCSP, AWS Security Specialty, AZ-500.
  • GRC (Governance, Risk, Compliance) — Policy, risk management, and auditing. Certifications: CISM, CRISC.

The CISSP: The Senior-Level Goal

The Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) is the premier certification for senior cybersecurity roles. It requires five years of paid work experience, covers eight domains of security, and is consistently listed as the most valuable certification in salary surveys. Average salaries for CISSP holders exceed $120,000 USD in the United States.

Treat CISSP as a long-term target — typically three to five years into your career. Many practitioners earn SSCP (Systems Security Certified Practitioner) first as an associate-level stepping stone.

"By 2025, the global cybersecurity workforce gap will exceed 3.5 million unfilled positions. Every qualified professional who enters the field today is stepping into extraordinary demand." — ISC² Cybersecurity Workforce Study
