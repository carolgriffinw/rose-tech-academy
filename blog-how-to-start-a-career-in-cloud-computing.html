How to Start a Career in Cloud Computing

Cloud Computing ROSE Tech Academy · 5 min read

Cloud computing is one of the fastest-growing and most in-demand fields in technology. Organizations of every size are migrating workloads to the cloud, and the demand for skilled cloud professionals consistently outpaces supply. That's good news if you're considering a career change or entering the tech industry for the first time.

Why Cloud Computing?

Cloud roles are among the highest-paying in technology, and many entry-level positions don't require a traditional computer science degree. What employers want is demonstrated knowledge — and that's exactly what cloud certifications prove.

According to the Global Knowledge IT Skills and Salary Report, cloud certifications are consistently among the top-paying IT credentials. More importantly, the skills are practical, learnable, and transferable across industries.

Did you know? The average cloud architect salary in the United States exceeds $150,000 per year. Entry-level cloud support roles typically start between $55,000–$75,000, with rapid growth potential as you earn certifications.

Start With an Entry-Level Certification

The three major cloud providers each offer a foundational certification designed for beginners:

  • Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) — Great for professionals in non-technical roles who work alongside engineering teams.
  • AWS Cloud Practitioner — AWS is the market leader, making this cert widely recognized by employers.
  • Google Cloud Digital Leader — Ideal if you're interested in data and AI-adjacent roles.

All three exams can be passed with 4–8 weeks of dedicated study, and they cost between $100–$200 each. Many learners use free or low-cost resources like Microsoft Learn, AWS Skill Builder, or Google Cloud Skills Boost to prepare.

Core Skills to Develop

Beyond certifications, employers want candidates who can speak fluently about:

  • Compute services — virtual machines, containers, serverless functions
  • Storage — blob/object storage, databases, file systems
  • Networking — virtual networks, load balancers, DNS, VPNs
  • Security basics — identity and access management, encryption, compliance
  • Pricing models — pay-as-you-go, reserved instances, cost optimization

You don't need to be an expert in all of these to get started. Your first certification will introduce each concept, and hands-on labs will deepen your understanding.

Entry-Level Job Roles

Once you have a foundational certification, you can begin applying for roles such as:

  • Cloud Support Associate
  • IT Help Desk Technician (cloud-focused)
  • Junior Cloud Administrator
  • Cloud Operations Analyst
  • DevOps Apprentice or Associate

Many of these roles prioritize certifications, attitude, and problem-solving ability over years of experience. A strong portfolio of lab projects and a passing score on a major exam can genuinely open doors.

Your Next Steps

If you're ready to begin, start by choosing one cloud platform — Microsoft Azure, AWS, or Google Cloud — and commit to its foundational certification. Use free official learning resources, supplement with practice exams, and schedule your exam within 60–90 days to maintain momentum.
