How to Write a Technology Resume With No Experience

Career ROSE Tech Academy · 2025-03-15 · 6 min read

Every experienced technology professional started somewhere. The challenge of writing a resume with no industry experience is real, but it is a solvable problem — and the solution is not to exaggerate or fabricate. It is to translate the genuine experience you do have into language that makes recruiters and hiring managers take notice.

1. Lead With a Strong Summary

Replace the generic "Objective" statement with a targeted professional summary (two to three sentences). This is your first impression. A good summary for a career-changer reads like: "Detail-oriented professional transitioning into cloud computing, backed by an AZ-900 certification and hands-on Azure lab experience. Experienced in process optimisation and cross-functional collaboration from five years in operations management. Seeking a Cloud Support or Cloud Administrator role where I can apply both technical and business skills."

Notice what this summary does: it acknowledges the transition honestly, highlights the certification, references a relevant transferable skill, and names a target role. Keep it under four lines.

2. List Your Certifications Prominently

Certifications are credentials — treat them like degrees. Create a dedicated "Certifications" section near the top of your resume (below the summary but before experience). Include:

  • Full certification name
  • Issuing organisation
  • Date earned (or "In Progress — expected [month/year]")
  • Credential ID or verification link if available

An in-progress certification is worth listing. It signals commitment and gives you something to discuss in an interview.

3. Build a Projects Section

If you lack work experience in tech, projects are your substitute. A well-crafted project entry demonstrates initiative, technical capability, and the ability to complete something. For each project, include:

  • A clear title and one-sentence description
  • Technologies used (list them explicitly — ATS systems scan for keywords)
  • What you built, why you built it, and what it demonstrates
  • A link to GitHub, a live demo, or a write-up
📁 Project Ideas for Common Tech Paths

Cloud: Deployed a three-tier web application on Azure using App Service, SQL Database, and Blob Storage. Cybersecurity: Completed 25 rooms on TryHackMe; built a home lab running Kali Linux and Windows Server VMs. Data: Analysed a public dataset using Python and Power BI; published findings to GitHub.

4. Reframe Existing Experience

Your non-tech work experience is more relevant than you think. Look for transferable elements:

  • Did you use or implement any software systems? Mention them.
  • Did you work with data, reports, or spreadsheets? Quantify the impact.
  • Did you train colleagues, manage processes, or solve technical problems? These are relevant.
  • Did you automate anything, even with Excel macros? Mention it.

Rewrite bullet points using the format: Action verb + task + outcome. "Reduced monthly reporting time by 40% by creating automated Excel dashboards for a team of 12" is far stronger than "Created reports for the team."

5. Optimise for Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)

Most companies use ATS software to filter resumes before a human ever sees them. Your resume must pass the filter first. Key practices:

  • Use a clean, single-column format (tables and columns confuse ATS parsers)
  • Mirror the exact language from the job posting in your resume
  • Spell out acronyms at least once (e.g., "Azure Virtual Machines (VMs)")
  • Save as .docx or PDF — check the job posting for preferences
  • Include a "Technical Skills" section listing technologies, platforms, and tools explicitly

6. Get Your Resume Reviewed

Before submitting, have your resume reviewed by at least one person in the technology industry. LinkedIn is a good place to find mentors willing to offer feedback. The ROSE Tech Academy community and resources like Blind, Reddit's r/cscareerquestions, and local tech meetups are also valuable feedback sources.

Also run your resume through a free ATS checker like Jobscan or Resume Worded to identify missing keywords.
